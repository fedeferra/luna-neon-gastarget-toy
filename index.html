Straggling in the neon target

A proton or alpha beam crosses the LUNA ²²Ne gas target (standard profile at 1 mbar, obtained from the profile measured at 2 mbar; measured pressure and temperature profiles: connecting tube, AP_u, chamber, AP_d, beam stop) and hits a resonance of ²²Ne(p,γ)²³Na or, with alphas, of ²²Ne(α,γ)²⁶Mg. Choose the resonance and change density, energies, width, current and detector to see where the reactions occur and where they are detected.

Photon lines: primary transitions from the ²²Ne(p,γ)²³Na ENSDF dataset (NNDC) and, for the 156.2 and 189.5 keV resonances, the branching ratios of Depalo et al., PRC 94, 055804 (2016); secondary transitions computed from the ²³Na adopted levels (NNDC, relative γ intensities, internal conversion neglected, levels without decay data not followed). Intensities are photons per resonance decay, so their sum exceeds 100 %. Resonances without published primary branching have no list. Each photon is treated as independent: no cascade summing in the detector. Simplified model. Density from p(z) and T(z) measured upstream of the chamber for ²²Ne at 2 mbar, divided by 2 to obtain the standard profile at 1 mbar (Ferraro et al., EPJA 54, 2018, Fig. 2), mirrored downstream about the chamber centre (z = 0), with identical AP_u and AP_d (3.93 cm); the gas is truncated at z_stop. Protons: Bethe stopping power with a correction fitted to the values used in the thesis (14.72 · 14.07 · 12.91 × 10⁻¹⁵ eV cm²/atom at 156.2 · 189.5 · 259.7 keV) and Bohr straggling with Z₁ = 1, Z₂ = 10. Alphas: stopping power obtained from the proton at equal velocity with the Ziegler effective charge, normalized to 32.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ eV cm² (centre of mass system) at Eα = 395 keV, the SRIM value used in Piatti et al., EPJA 58 (2022) 194. The alpha Bohr straggling at 395 keV through the 10.6 cm of the chamber at 1 mbar is about 1.5 keV, in agreement with the 1.4 keV value of that paper. Proton strengths: Ferraro PhD thesis 2017 (Tab. 5.1) up to 661 keV, then Iliadis et al., NPA 841 (2010) 251, which takes them from Endt 1990; at 661 keV the thesis gives 0.032 eV, Iliadis 0.35 eV and a recent preprint 0.472 eV; at 850.9 keV Iliadis gives 7.0 eV and Shahina et al. 2022 give 9.2 ± 0.7 eV. Alpha strengths: ≤ 4.0 × 10⁻¹¹ eV at 395 keV (LUNA, Piatti 2022), 2.8 ± 0.8 × 10⁻¹⁰ eV at 554 keV (Jayatissa 2020), upper limit ≤ 1.5 × 10⁻⁷ eV at 650 keV and (35 ± 2) × 10⁻⁶ eV at 830 keV (Shahina 2022, CASPAR). The other (α,γ) resonances below 1 MeV are not tabulated in the sources consulted: use "Custom". Total widths are not reported, so Γ is 0.1 keV for illustration. Values marked ≤ are upper limits. Yield per proton or alpha Y = (λ²/2) ωγ (M+m)/M ∫ n pdf dz. Beam heating: density reduction proportional to the beam power (current × energy) over the whole profile, uniform along z; the default value, 6% for 250 µA at 100 keV (25 W) in 2 mbar of ²²Ne, is the correction of EPJA 54 (2018) 44 for this chamber, and is also used for alphas. Detector: always centred on the chamber. Beam tube (Ø 20 / 24 mm): geometry only, excluding the gas density; the fraction of beam intercepted is estimated from the beam width (radial Gaussian) at a radius of 10 mm. Chamber length (default 20 cm; the Ferraro et al. measurement is at 10.6 cm): the ends, with AP_u and AP_d, move symmetrically and the measured profile moves with them. Detector efficiency: own Monte Carlo simulation (10⁶ photons per point) of isotropic photons emitted by point sources on the axis, for 28 energies between 20 keV and 20 MeV (between 0 and 20 keV it scales linearly to zero, below the absorption of the tube; interpolation in ln E) and 21 z positions between 0 and 30 cm (symmetric in z). Physics: photoelectric effect, Compton scattering with the Klein and Nishina cross section, pair production with annihilation at rest and tracking of the 511 keV photons; cross sections from xraylib up to 780 keV (photoelectric effect and Compton with binding), then Klein and Nishina, Bethe and Heitler parametrization for pairs (Geant4 type formula, verified on Pb and O at 10 MeV within 1%, applied up to 20 MeV) and power law extrapolation for the photoelectric effect; Rayleigh scattering is neglected and above about 10 MeV photonuclear production (giant resonance) is not included, nor is the escape of electrons and bremsstrahlung photons, which beyond a few MeV lowers the real peak efficiency compared with the simulated one. Secondary electrons deposit their energy locally, except the radiative fraction (bremsstrahlung), re-emitted as an isotropic photon of mean energy; there is no escape of K X rays or electrons. Peak: the energy deposited in the whole detector is smeared with a Gaussian of FWHM(E) (7% at 662 keV for NaI, 10% for BGO, 2 keV at 1332 keV for HPGe, scaling as √E, evaluated at the deposited energy), and an event counts in the peak if the smeared energy lies in the symmetric window E ± FWHM(E); this includes about 98% of the full energy Gaussian and excludes events with partial energy loss (escape of bremsstrahlung photons or Compton scattered photons) larger than about one FWHM. The total efficiency counts any deposit above 0.5 keV. Check: for the BGO and NaI arrays the simulated total efficiency lies between the analytic first interaction bounds (with and without the beam tube) at 50 keV to 1 MeV, and the geometric coverage from z = 0 is 87.2% (NaI) and 97.6% (BGO) of 4π; at 1 MeV the simulated total efficiency exceeds the no tube bound by 0.5% (BGO) and 3% (NaI), which is explained by photons scattered in the tube wall into the detector. Geometries: NaI is an array of six crystals of 10 × 10 × 20 cm (long axis parallel to the beam) in a brick arrangement around a square 10 × 10 cm bore, centred on z = 0: the two upper and the two lower crystals touch over whole faces, each of the two middle crystals touches half of an upper and half of a lower face; the crystals are in contact, with no gaps or dead layers, and the efficiency is that of the summed signal of all six (addback), so a photon scattered from one crystal to another is fully counted (the efficiency of a single crystal is lower). BGO is an array of six prisms with trapezoidal bases (height 7 cm), in contact along their oblique sides, forming a cylinder with a hexagonal through bore along the beam axis (inscribed circle of radius 3 cm, so the outer hexagon has an apothem of 10 cm), 28 cm long and centred on z = 0; as for NaI the crystals are in contact with no dead layers and the efficiency is that of the summed signal (the orientation of the hexagon, with flat faces up and down, and the 28 cm length are assumptions); HPGe (assumed dimensions) cylinder of 8 cm diameter and 8 cm length with axis perpendicular to the beam and face at 4 cm from the axis; the beam tube (stainless steel, inner Ø 20 mm, outer 24 mm, 2 mm thick, assumed material) crosses the bore and attenuates and scatters the photons; reaction chamber, supports and shields are absent. A single photon of energy E_γ is simulated: for cascades of several photons the 4π detectors measure a sum peak, with higher efficiency, which this model does not compute. Resonance: Breit and Wigner profile (Lorentzian of total width Γ, brought to the laboratory with (M+m)/M) convolved with the Gaussian beam energy distribution, that is a Voigt profile. Energy straggling (improved model): Bohr variance Ω² = 4π e⁴ Z₁² Z₂ N with effective charge for alphas, reduced at low velocity with a Lindhard and Scharff type factor (formula not verified against a primary source; the simple model shows the effect), and propagated along the path with dσ²/dn = Ω²(E) − 2 ε'(E) σ², where ε' is the derivative of the stopping power; non Gaussian tails and the excess of straggling near the stopping power maximum are not included. Angular straggling (improved model): Gaussian core of Molière multiple scattering with Thomas and Fermi screening, with energy and thickness varying along the path; for these thicknesses the Molière parameter b is small (few scatterings), so single scattering tails are not represented; initial divergence 0.5 mrad, initial size 1 mm. Model limits: beam heating is linear in the beam power (an extrapolation beyond the 25 W measured) and uniform in z; the beam has energy Eb at the start of the domain (z = −(Lchamber/2 + 25.7) cm) and the gas upstream of that point is neglected; the apertures AP_u and AP_d do not cut the beam in the calculation; the alpha stopping power is accurate to about 10%. Effect studied in Bemmerer et al., EPL 122 (2018) 52001.
